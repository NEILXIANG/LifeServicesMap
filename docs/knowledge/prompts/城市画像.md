# Part A — people and economy

You are researching a CITY PROFILE of <CITY>, <PROVINCE> for a free multilingual guide used by people who have just
moved there. The profile decides what the guide must emphasise for this city, so every figure must come from an
official source, with its URL and reference year.

Source priority (strict):
1. government — national statistics office (Statistics Canada Census Profile for the city and its metropolitan area;
   latest population estimates), provincial statistics office, national housing agency (CMHC Rental Market Report),
   provincial government;
2. official regional bodies — the city, the regional district/county, the economic development commission, the airport;
3. only if nothing official exists: reputable institutions (university, chamber of commerce) — label these.
Never invent a number. If a value cannot be found officially, write "not found" and what you tried.

Collect, for <CITY> and (where available) its metropolitan area and neighbouring municipalities in scope:
1. Population: last census, the one before (growth %), latest official estimate.
2. Age: median age; share 65+; share 15–24.
3. Immigration: share of immigrants; recent immigrants (last 5 years) and their top places of birth; non-permanent
   residents (temporary workers, international students).
4. Languages: top non-official languages spoken most often at home (top 8, with counts).
5. Visible minority / ethnic origin highlights.
6. Indigenous identity share.
7. Economy: main industries by employment (top 6), major employers, latest unemployment rate, median household income.
8. Cost of living: average rent (1- and 2-bedroom) and vacancy rate from the latest official survey.
9. Seasonal or temporary workforce (agriculture, tourism) — official sources only.
10. Students: enrolment and international share at each post-secondary campus, from the institutions' own fact pages.
For comparison, collect items 1, 2, 3 and 8 for <REFERENCE CITY>.

Write JSON {"<city>": {...}, "<reference city>": {...}, "notes": [...]} where every value is
{"value", "year", "source_url", "source_name"}, to <OUT_FILE>. **Write the file early and update it as you go.**
Reply with the most decision-relevant differences from <REFERENCE CITY> and everything you could not find officially.

# Part B — place, culture and services

Same purpose and source rule (government first; then the institutions' own sites; never a directory or an AI summary;
"not found" rather than a guess; every item with a URL). Collect:
1. Geography: water, city shape, bridges and highways that structure travel; the municipalities in the region and which
   services each runs (transit, libraries, recreation).
2. Indigenous peoples: whose territory; local Nations and their governments; cultural sites a visitor can go to; the
   city's own land acknowledgement wording. (These pages will be reviewed by the Nation before launch — L-068.)
3. History relevant to people arriving now (immigrant communities' histories) — museum or government sources only.
4. Culture and identity: what the city is known for, cultural district, museums, major annual events.
5. Climate: official climate normals (January and July means, precipitation, snowfall, hot days).
6. Hazards: wildfire, smoke, flood, snow/ice, extreme heat or cold — history from official sources and **how residents
   get alerts today** (the current official system and its sign-up page).
7. Gateways: airport (passengers, routes) and **how to get from the airport to downtown by transit, by day of week**;
   intercity bus; rail.
8. Transit: operator, number of routes, rapid/express lines, fare system, municipalities served, GTFS feed URL.
9. Education: school district(s) (number of schools, French immersion, English-language-learner support, welcome or
   intake centre), francophone schools, post-secondary campuses and where they are.
10. Health: health authority, hospitals, urgent and primary care centres, how to find a family doctor.
11. Settlement: every settlement agency and who it serves (by immigration status), from the national directory of
    settlement services and the agencies' own sites.
12. Official open data: the city's open data portal and the datasets useful for a guide (parks, facilities, business
    licences), with their licence.
Write JSON to <OUT_FILE> as in Part A, early and often. Reply with what makes <CITY> different from <REFERENCE CITY>
and what the guide must therefore do differently.
