Kamloops Life Guide — a working tool for settlement teams

Prepared for Kamloops Immigrant Services · data version 2026.09.24b · places checked 2026-09-11 · task cards checked 2026-09-08

What it is: one web page that answers the two questions your counsellors hear all day — where is it (387 Kamloops places on a map, each showing where its information came from) and how do I do this (17 step-by-step cards in six languages). No account, no download, free, works on a phone, works offline.
What it is not: not immigration advice, not a referral database that replaces your intake, and not a service that collects anything about your clients.

2. Why it may save your team time

  • The client can keep going after the appointment. The same card is in their language on their own phone, with the steps they have already done ticked off.
  • Print it and hand it over. Any single card prints on one page for a client who has no data plan or no phone.
  • Fewer repeat explanations of the same eight or ten processes, in whichever language the client reads.
  • Nothing to install or administer, no logins to manage for staff or clients.

3. Languages, and the honest gap

  • Full six-language coverage — English, French, Spanish, Chinese, Korean, Japanese — for the interface, all 17 cards, and the notes on 360 of the 387 places.
  • A search works whatever language the interface is set to: someone reading English can type 超市 and still find the supermarkets. 743 everyday search terms across six languages are built in.
  • Not covered yet: Punjabi, Tagalog, Arabic, Ukrainian. Adding a language is a translation job, not a rebuild — the structure already supports it.

4. Client safety and privacy

  • No account, no name, email or phone collected, no cookies, no IP logged. One tap clears everything stored on that device — usable on a shared computer, at a shelter, or on a borrowed phone.
  • Search words about suicide, family violence or assault — in any of the six languages — bring up the crisis lines immediately, including the 24-hour Indigenous line and 9-8-8.
  • Cards state plainly that care is given regardless of immigration status, that what a client tells a nurse is confidential, and that applying to the Residential Tenancy Branch is a legal right.
  • "Near me" keeps the client's location on their own device; it is never sent anywhere.

5. How the information is kept honest

  • Every record shows its source — the organisation's own website (251), the Province of British Columbia's open data (80), or third-party map data marked "please call to confirm" (56) — and the month it was listed.
  • If it cannot be verified, it is not published. 47 phone numbers that could not be matched to an official source were removed rather than left to be wrong.
  • Links go to the exact page (a specific library branch, a specific store), not a home page. 22 places whose exact address is uncertain say "approximate location".
  • Opening hours are structured data, so "open now" is correct on statutory holidays too. Where nobody publishes them — elementary schools and most licensed child care — the card says "Hours not listed — call to confirm" instead of a number no one checked.
  • Every change runs through an automated check suite before it goes live, and every place and card has a "report an issue" link.
