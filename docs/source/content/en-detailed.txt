@h2 1. The problem it answers
@p Someone who has just arrived in Kamloops has to do a dozen things in an order nobody tells them, at offices they cannot picture, with documents they may not have — and often in a second language. Official websites are accurate but written for people who already know the vocabulary; friends and forums are friendly but out of date. Front-line staff at the City, the library, the settlement agency and the health authority answer the same questions every day, one person at a time.
@p Kamloops Life Guide puts those answers on one page that fits in a pocket. It is deliberately narrow: not everything about Kamloops, only the things that stop a household from functioning when they are missing. The aim throughout is to make an already stressful season a little less so — accurate rather than clever, and patient about the fact that everything here is unfamiliar at first.

@h3 The guide: {TASKS} task cards
@p Each card starts from the resident's own question rather than the program's name, and answers it the same way every time: why it matters · the steps · what to bring · where to go (address, phone, hours, map pin) · cost · how long it takes · the deadline · what to say at the counter · what to do if you get stuck · the official source it was checked against, with the date. A card can be ticked as done, shared as a link, printed on one page, or (where there is a deadline) added to a phone calendar.

@h2 3. How it is made and kept honest
@marks
**Sources.** Task cards are written from the official program pages (Service Canada, Service BC / ICBC, Health Insurance BC, SD73, CRA, the Residential Tenancy Branch, the BC Human Rights Tribunal, Legal Aid BC…) and re-read against them; the check date is printed at the foot of every card. Place facts come from the organisations' own websites and Google Places; each record shows its own date.
**Verification rules.** A place whose hours cannot be established is shown as "hours unknown", never as "open" or "closed". {APPROX} places whose exact building could not be confirmed from two independent sources are flagged "approximate location" and navigation uses the street address, not the pin. Records that could not be confirmed at all are held back rather than shown.
**Corrections.** Anyone — a resident, an organisation that is listed, a City department — can email a correction. Errors are fixed, and an organisation that does not wish to be listed is removed, within five working days.
**Freshness.** The whole set was last checked on {VERIFIED}; the version number ({VERSION}) is printed in the page footer so a printed card can be matched to what was live that day.

@h2 4. Privacy, security and legal position
@cards
No personal data | No account, no sign-up, no cookies used to identify anyone, no location stored. "Near me" asks the phone once and keeps nothing. Favourites and ticked cards stay in the reader's own browser.
Anonymous usage data only | Which places and features were used, and the words typed in search (digits stripped, truncated). It carries a random identifier replaced every 90 days, sets no cookie, stores no IP, and cannot be linked to a person. No advertising, no third-party trackers; the visit counter is Cloudflare's cookie-free one.
Not an official source | The page says so on every screen: a personal reference, not published by any government or organisation, not legal, medical, immigration or tax advice. In an emergency it says: call 911.
Open licence | Text and structure are released under Creative Commons BY-NC 4.0: the City, libraries, schools and non-profits may print, copy and adapt it freely with attribution. Facts (addresses, hours) are not claimed as anyone's property.
@note Hosting: Cloudflare (Canada/global edge). The page sends a strict Content-Security-Policy, cannot be embedded inside another website, and works offline once loaded (it can be installed to a phone's home screen). It is governed by the laws of British Columbia.

@h2 5. Ways it can be used — all free
@table
Setting | How it helps
Front counters, 311, reception | Hand over a link, a QR flyer (Letter/A4, black-and-white friendly, already designed) or a printed card instead of re-explaining the steps. The card is one page and in the resident's language.
Welcome packages, libraries, community centres | Insert the flyer; the QR code opens the guide in the phone's language. No app store, no download.

A scoped edition | The page can be built as a scoped edition — only the categories or area an organisation serves, with its own name and contact line on the page — without changing the underlying data.
Feedback loop | If a partner organisation spots an error or a change (a moved office, new hours), one email fixes it for every language at once.
